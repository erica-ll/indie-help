Scale A — Answerable questions
Start by placing the answer in a band on completeness, then apply caps and deductions.

10 — Complete and exact. Every required chunk retrieved (all hops, all documents). Every load-bearing specific preserved: figures, proper nouns, named lists. Answers the question actually asked, not the topic near it. Correct attribution. Nothing added that isn't in the source. If the source is partial, the answer says where it stops.

9 — Complete, one cosmetic flaw. All required content present. One non-load-bearing omission, or one minor overstatement that would not change the developer's decision.

8 — Correct but less useful. Nothing wrong. Loses one differentiating specific — a named list, a verifiable figure, or the question's exact referent. The developer could act on it but would have to ask a follow-up.

7 — Right verdict, incomplete evidence. One required hop or document missing. The system compensates with its own reasoning rather than fabricating, and lands the correct conclusion anyway.

6 — Directionally right, operationally useless. Missing hop and thin on concrete support — true but unusable as ammunition. Or: complete retrieval hedged into a non-answer.

5 — Half the question. The missing hop is the half the user specifically asked about. Or the answer partially validates a premise the corpus contradicts.

4 — Relevant retrieval, wrong synthesis. Found the right neighborhood; mischaracterized the source's position, resolved an ambiguous term to the wrong sense, or drew a conclusion the chunks don't support.

3 — Generic. Plausible advice not traceable to the corpus. Citations present, content interchangeable with an ungrounded LLM reply.

2 — False refusal. Declined a directly answerable question. Scored above 1 and 0 deliberately: it's honest, causes no downstream damage, and is cheap to diagnose.

1 — Confidently wrong. Wrong on the central fact, but the error is traceable to the corpus — a misread, not an invention.

0 — Fabrication or leakage. An entity, quote, figure, or study that isn't in the corpus, presented as sourced. Also: supplying pretrained specifics on a facet the corpus doesn't cover. Fluency and real-world accuracy are irrelevant here — the failure is unsourced assertion.

Condition	Cap
Fabricated entity / quote / figure	0
Any claim attributed to the wrong source document	5
Citation attached to a claim absent from the cited chunk ("decorative citation")	6
Required second document missing (cross-doc question)	6
Fully endorses a premise the corpus contradicts	6
Hedged non-answer to a decision question ("balance both," "consider each")	6
Required second chunk missing within one document (multi-hop)	7

Deductions (apply after the cap, −1 each, floor at the band below)
Loses a load-bearing figure, named list, or exact proper noun
Overstates scope of a source claim (e.g. presenting a claim the source scopes to one level or scene as applying to the whole game). NOTE: do not apply this to "all the textures were painted in Substance Painter" - the Roottrees source itself says "The textures in the game are all painted in Substance Painter", so that phrasing is faithful.
Conflates two entities' properties into one claim
Answers the topic but never names the question's specific referent
Passing concession to a contradicted premise (as opposed to a full endorsement, which caps at 6)

Bonuses (+1, cannot exceed 10)
Corrects a false premise in the question instead of playing along
States explicitly what the source does not cover, unprompted
Correctly withholds an available-but-inapplicable detail because the user already ruled it out


Scale B — Abstention questions
10 — Graded refusal. Declines the unanswerable part, states plainly that it isn't in the corpus, and offers whatever adjacent material does exist.

9 — Clean refusal. Declines correctly; nothing partial was available to offer.

8 — Blunt refusal. Declines correctly when a genuinely useful partial answer existed and went unoffered.

5 — Hedged refusal. "The source doesn't detail this, but typically…" — this is where leakage begins. The disclaimer doesn't neutralize the specifics that follow.

2 — Leaks generalities. Supplies uncited pretrained content after acknowledging the gap.

0 — Leaks specifics as sourced. Delivers pretrained facts under a citation.

The unearned-pass rule
An abstention only demonstrates discipline if the relevant document is actually indexed. If retrieval returned nothing, the system had no temptation to resist. Mark these "10 (unverified)" and confirm ingestion before counting them as evidence. Your Q3 is a verified pass — the handbook was demonstrably indexed. Your Q11 is unverified.
